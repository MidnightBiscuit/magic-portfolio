## Overview

In this project, I was tasked with designing a new miniature trap for Ca<sup>+</sup> ions coupled with a driven cantilever. A previous experimental system allowed to do a proof-of-concept and obtain the first scientific results in the classical regime of the interaction. Nonetheless, the trap itself had reached its limitations and could not operate in the semi-quantum and quantum regimes. The need for a new robust and stable trap motivated this project. The main challenge was to accomodate for a cantilever assembly in a miniaturised trap. We have learned a lot from the first prototype and designed a new trap that would keep as much of its advantages while allieviating its drawbacks.

## Key Features

- **Fine control of the trapping potential**: the trapping potential is a combination of the potential generated by the trap and the potential generated by the charged cantilever. The cantilever being movable it is essential the potential of the trap can be finely adjusted for every configuration.
- **Complete compensation scheme**: the trapping potential may be altered by several factors : trap imperfections, patch potentials, impedence and capacitances mismatches. A propoer compensation scheme should be implemented.
- **Room for a vertical cantilever and optical access**: a bigger trap would be less sensitive to perturbations, allow for a better optical access and minimise the effect of patch potentials. Nonetheless the dimensions should remain compact enough so a cantilever assembly could be fitted.

## Technologies Used

- **Numerical modelisation with Finite Element Method (COMSOL, Python)**: electrode design was numerically explored using COMSOL. Electrode dimensions, spacing and voltage was explored systematically in order to maximise trap depth and reach the desired anharmonicity.
- **Computer-aided design (SolidWorks)**: the final trap design was drawn after numerical optimisation of the merit-function.
- **Laser etching**: for the production laser etching of glass substrate was choosen due to its precision affordability. The glass can be gold plated and assembeled on the experimental platform.

## Challenges and Learnings

### The right geometry
It is very important to design trap that would be both robust and compact, providing a stable potential with limited distortions from a perfect quadrupole, and that electric compensation of imperfections can be applied with all required degrees of freedom. The classical linear quadrupole geometry based on segmented rods or blade would satisfy those constraits quite well. Nevertheless the behaviour of the cantilever assembly depends on its length and mass and unfortunately the classic geometry lacks compacticity. The stacked wafer geometry would be the closest from the classical geometry while being much more compact on the vertical axis. The challenge was to ensure we would keep most of the advantages of this geometry while miniaturising it to accomodate for the cantilever assembly. The purpose of the numerical simulations has been to optimise the design so the shape of the potential and the level of anharmonicity wouldbe satisfying. The position of the minimum of the trap has been studied position as a function of the position and voltage of the cantilever.

### Electric supply and compensation
Also to keep the electric supply simpler and avoid overheating the radio-frequency and dc power supply could have been separated (rf could be fed to a single pair of diagonal electrdes while the dc could be fed to the other pair). But for the electric compensation we would need to apply dc to all electrodes to have all degrees of freedom required. It has been decided to have rf applied to both pair of diagonal electrodes wit the dc superimposed. This would also divide by two the required rf amplitude, partly compensating for the doubling of the radius with respect to the current geometry, at the cost of a more complex power supply management. This scheme avoids additionnal electrodes located around the trap for the compensation and that would be, first of all quite challenging to place precisely with the choosen geometry, second annoying regarding optical and mechanical access.

### Electrode etching
Finally, all electrodes being on the same gold coated glass substrate it is necessary to ensure electrical insulation of all electrodes. Taking advantage of the precision of laser etching, this is done by digging inverted T-shaped trenches in between electrodes. This way the gold cannot reach the inner part of the trenches, effectively creating a discontinuity in the coating.
